### L01 · D0 · Launch
**EN**
Today we opened Atlas.

Atlas is a branded academy platform for Egyptian teachers who sell their lessons online. Many of them already run a real business on three tools: a WhatsApp group, YouTube links and a growing pile of transfer screenshots. Atlas gives that business one place with the teacher's name on it.

Each academy gets a website on its own Atlas subdomain, in Arabic and English. Students pay the teacher directly by Vodafone Cash, Orange Cash, Etisalat Cash, WE Pay, InstaPay or bank transfer, upload the proof, and the account owner approves it once to enrol them. Atlas takes no commission on those payments. Assistants join as managers or instructors, and one account can run more than one academy, as the plan allows.

Two limits we state up front: approval is manual and done by the account owner, and there is no card or Fawry checkout today.

If you run a teaching team in Egypt, there is a 14-day trial with no card needed. Link in the first comment.

### L02 · D3 · Several brands, one account
**EN**
Some teaching businesses in Egypt run more than one name: a brand per subject, per star teacher, or per stage. Each name usually ends up with its own tools and its own logins.

On Atlas, each name is its own academy with its own website, its own students and its own payment methods, all under one account. A student who joins one academy has an account in that academy only and does not appear in the others. Each academy's students and courses are kept separate in the database, and we have checked that separation in production.

The Organization plan covers up to 5 academies, 1,500 students and 25 instructors [check live price at launch]. If you run more than that, or you need different terms, we would rather talk it through than guess.

### L03 · D10 · Who sees what in a teaching team
**EN**
When a teacher adds assistants to an online academy, the first question is who can see what.

On Atlas there are three roles. The account owner sees the whole account and is the only role that approves student payments. A manager helps run the academy day to day. An instructor sees and grades only the courses assigned to them: their quizzes, with five question types and manual grading where an answer needs a person, and their homework, with due dates, a late policy and written feedback.

We kept payment approval with the owner on purpose, because the money belongs to the teacher whose name is on the academy. The cost of that choice is that an assistant cannot approve a payment on the owner's behalf today, and we say so before anyone signs up.

If you are planning roles for a team, the link to talk to us is in the first comment.

### L04 · D17 · Payments that go straight to the academy
**EN**
Most teachers in Egypt who sell online are paid by wallet or InstaPay, then confirm each transfer by hand from a screenshot.

Atlas keeps the same payment habits and moves the proof into the academy. The student picks one of the methods the academy added (Vodafone Cash, Orange Cash, Etisalat Cash, WE Pay, InstaPay or bank transfer), uploads the receipt with its reference, and the owner approves it once. The student is enrolled and emailed. If something is wrong, the owner rejects it with a reason and the student can pay again. Every order and payment sits in one list with its proof and status.

The money goes straight to the teacher. Atlas takes no commission on these payments and charges only for the plan.

What it does not do yet: approval is manual and done by the account owner, there is no card or Fawry checkout today, and revenue charts do not include these manual payments yet, so the lists are the record.

Plans and limits are linked in the first comment.

### L05 · D24 · What we don't do yet, and why we say it first
**EN**
Every Atlas feature page says where the feature stops. Here is the short list of what Atlas does not offer today: a mobile app (academies run in the phone browser), card or Fawry checkout, live classes, video file uploads, payments that approve themselves, and WhatsApp or SMS messages to students or parents.

One more limit we repeat wherever lessons come up: lessons are YouTube videos played inside your academy, and a determined student can still copy the YouTube link itself. No website can fully stop screen recording, so we don't promise it.

We publish this list because a teacher who moves paid students and then finds a missing piece pays for that surprise in front of their students. We would rather lose the sign-up than cause that.

If the list above doesn't block you, the 14-day trial needs no card. Link in the first comment.
